Humanity wants to reach another planet while the only one that can keep us alive is getting hotter, dirtier and emptier. This site shows the biggest threats we created, what we can do if we act together, and why the smartest civilization is not the one that takes the most energy, but the one that needs the least.

Part 1

The threats we made ourselves

None of these came from space or from a volcano. They come from how we make energy, grow food, travel and throw things away. Click "Read more" on a card.

Climate change

1.2°C+

Earth is already about 1.2°C hotter than before the industrial age, and CO2 in the air is above 420 parts per million.

Burning coal, oil and gas traps heat. The result is stronger heat waves, floods, droughts and sea level rise. Coastal cities and farming regions suffer first.

Loss of species

1 million

Scientists estimate about one million animal and plant species are at risk of extinction.

Forests are cut, wetlands drained and oceans overfished. When insects, pollinators and fish disappear, our own food supply becomes weaker.

Polluted oceans

8 million t

Around 8 million tonnes of plastic reach the oceans every year.

The ocean also absorbs extra CO2 and becomes more acidic. Coral reefs, shellfish and the fish that feed billions of people are at risk.

Water shortage

2.2 billion

About 2.2 billion people still do not have safely managed drinking water.

Glaciers are melting, rivers are polluted and groundwater is pumped faster than it refills. Water shortage can lead to hunger and migration.

Dirty air

7 million

Air pollution is linked to about 7 million early deaths each year.

Smoke from vehicles, factories and burning waste harms lungs and hearts. The same fuels that pollute air also cause climate change, so one fix helps both.

Tired soil and food

1/3

About one third of the world's soil is already degraded.

Over-farming, chemicals and deforestation turn fertile land into dust. A growing population needs more food from less healthy land.

The biggest future danger is that these threats feed each other: heat dries soil, dry soil lowers food, hunger creates conflict. Together they can break the systems that civilization depends on.

What if humanity works together?

Every solution already exists: solar and wind power, electric transport, forest restoration, recycling and smarter farming. What is missing is everyone doing it at the same time. Move the slider to see the idea.

Result

  • Food: waste less, eat more plants, protect soil.
  • Nature: plant and protect forests, wetlands and oceans.
  • Things: repair, reuse and recycle instead of throw away.

Part 3

Aiming for Mars, healing Earth

Space exploration is wonderful. But look at how hard Mars is compared with the planet we are damaging. Even our worst-case Earth is easier to live on than the best-case Mars.

Feature Earth (damaged) Mars (today)
Average temperature About 15°C About -60°C
Air 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen 95% carbon dioxide, almost no oxygen
Air pressure 1 (normal) About 0.6% of Earth's
Water Oceans, rivers, rain Ice at the poles and underground
Radiation shield Magnetic field and thick air Very weak
Journey You are already here 6 to 9 months, one way

The plan is not "Earth or Mars". Technology built for Mars, such as recycling every drop of water and growing food in closed systems, is exactly what Earth needs. Treat Earth as the practice planet: if we cannot keep one living world healthy, we cannot keep a new one healthy either.

Part 4

Why go small, not only big?

Scientists rank civilizations on the Kardashev scale by how much energy they use: Type 1 uses the energy of a planet, Type 2 of a star and Type 3 of a galaxy. Humanity is at about Type 0.7. This path means expanding: more energy, bigger machines, more space.

Physicist John D. Barrow suggested the opposite direction in 1998: a civilization can also advance by mastering smaller and smaller things, from objects our own size, to genes, to molecules, to atoms. Here these steps are called Type -1, -2 and -3. Instead of expanding, we use microbiology and small-scale science to repair the environment. Click a type.

Why the small scale has so much potential

  • It fixes the cause. Pollution is made of molecules. If we can break them down and rebuild them, we remove the waste instead of hiding it.
  • Life is the best technology. Microbes have recycled everything on Earth for about 3.5 billion years. Copying them needs very little energy.
  • It costs less than expanding. Type 1 needs roughly 500 times more power than we use today. A lab, a microscope and a bucket of bacteria need far less.
  • You can start now. A student can study biotechnology or chemistry today. Nobody can start building a Dyson sphere.
  • It needs care. Changing genes and releasing microbes must be tested and controlled, so that the cure does not become a new problem.
